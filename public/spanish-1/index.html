Unit 1: Greetings & Introductions

Buenos días
Good morning — used until roughly noon.
¿Cómo te llamas?
What's your name? (informal) — answers with Me llamo…
Mucho gusto
Nice to meet you.
Hasta mañana
See you tomorrow — hasta = until.
Ser (yo/tú)
soy, eres — to be for identity, origin, traits.
Usted
Formal 'you' — takes third-person verb forms (usted habla).
Tengo … años
I'm … years old — age uses tener, never ser.
Ser vs estar core
DOCTOR traits vs PLACE conditions.
Tú vs usted
Informal friend vs formal respect.
Greetings clock
buenos días tardes noches.

Unit 2: Numbers, Dates & Time

Veintiuno / treinta y uno
21 fuses into one word; 31+ needs y between tens and units.
El primero de mayo
May 1st — primero only for the first of the month.
Son las dos y media
It's 2:30 — media = half past; time uses ser.
Menos cuarto
Quarter TO the next hour: las cinco menos cuarto = 4:45.
¿Qué día es hoy?
What day is today? (vs ¿Cuál es la fecha? for the date)
De la mañana / tarde / noche
AM / afternoon / PM markers after telling time.
Gustar logic
'Is pleasing to' — me gusta(n).
Definite articles
el/la/los/las match gender+number.
Me llamo…
My name is — from llamarse.

Unit 3: Family & Descriptions

Los abuelos
Grandparents (abuelo + abuela, or mixed set).
Mi / mis · tu / tus · su / sus
Possessives agreeing in number with the thing owned.
Nuestro/a/os/as
Our — the only possessive that agrees in gender too.
Tener … años
To be … years old: Mi hermana tiene doce años.
Simpático/a
Nice, pleasant — the default compliment adjective.
Tener hambre / sed / miedo
To be hungry / thirsty / afraid — tener expressions.
El/la primo/a
Cousin — final -o masculine, -a feminine; primos = mixed set.
¿De quién es…?
Whose is it? — answers with Es de + person.
Possessives
mi/tu/su/nuestro agree with thing owned.
Adjective agreement
alto/alta/altos/altas copy the noun.
Tener expressions
tener hambre sed años.

Unit 4: School & -AR Verbs

-AR endings
o, as, a, amos, áis, an — hablar pattern for hundreds of verbs.
La mochila
Backpack — school-supply staple word.
Necesito + infinitive
I need TO…: Necesito estudiar. Second verb stays whole.
Siempre / a veces / nunca
Always / sometimes / never — frequency adverbs.
Escuchar vs mirar
Listen(to) vs watch/look(at): escuchar música, mirar la tele.
Terminar / tomar / trabajar
Finish / take-have / work — three everyday -AR verbs.
Pasar / preparar
To pass/spend time · to prepare — two workhorse -AR verbs.
La biblioteca
Library — cognate-friendly study place.
Tengo que + infinitive
I have TO…: Tengo que trabajar hoy.
Ir+a+infinitive
Voy a comer = going to eat.
Question words
qué quién cómo cuándo dónde por qué.
Colors agreement
rojo roja rojos rojas follow noun.

Unit 5: Food & -ER/-IR Verbs

-ER endings
o, es, e, emos, éis, en — comer: como, comes, come…
-IR nosotros/vosotros
-imos / -ís: vivimos, vivís — the only -ER/-IR difference.
El pescado
Fish as FOOD (el pez is alive in water).
Con / sin
With / without — un café con leche, sin azúcar.
La cuenta, por favor
The check, please — end-of-meal essential.
Compartir
To share (-IR): compartir el almuerzo con amigos.
El pollo
Chicken (food) — pollo asado, sopa de pollo.
Food phrases
tener hambre = to be hungry.
Meal words
desayuno almuerzo cena.

Unit 6: Gustar & Likes

Me gusta(n) + noun
I like it/them — gustar matches the THING's number.
Gusta + infinitive
Liking activities always singular gusta: Me gusta nadar.
Encantar
To love something — stronger than gustar, same grammar.
No me gusta nada
I don't like it at all — nada intensifies the negative.
A Juan le gusta…
Clarifier for ambiguous le: A Juan (le) likes…
Me interesa(n)
It interests me — gustar-family verb for topics.
-IR endings
-o -es -e -imos -en.
Places vocab
biblioteca parque cine tienda escuela.
-IR nosotros form
-imos differs from -ER -emos.

Unit 7: Stem-Changers & Clothing

Stem-changer rule
Stressed vowel swaps in all forms EXCEPT nosotros/vosotros (boot).
Pensar / querer / preferir
e→ie family: pienso, quiero, prefiero.
Poder / dormir / almorzar
o→ue family: puedo, duermo, almuerzo.
Jugar → juego
The lone u→ue verb — to play (sports/games).
¿Cuánto cuesta(n)?
How much does/do it cost? — agrees with the item.
Llevar
To wear OR to carry: Llevo una camisa azul.
Stem-change boot
e→ie o→ue except nosotros.
Clothes verbs
llevar wear · probarse try on.
Pensar que
pensar que = think that (no subjunctive).

Unit 8: House & Ser vs Estar

Estar (yo)
estoy — irregular yo; rest regular: estás, está…
Location → estar
Where things sit uses estar: La mesa está cerca.
Ser for events' venues
La fiesta ES en mi casa — event location breaks the estar rule.
Estoy estudiando
estar + -ando/-iendo = happening right now.
Del / al
de+el and a+el contractions — the only two in Spanish.
La planta baja
Ground floor — one floor below el primer piso.
Encima de / al lado de
On top of / next to — core location prepositions.
O→UE examples
poder→puedo dormir→duermo.
Weather phrases
hace calor está lloviendo hay niebla.
Hace + time
hace dos años = two years ago.
¿Cómo se dice…?
How do you say…? — asking for translations.

Unit 1: Greetings & Introductions

Basic greetings
Spanish greetings change with time of day and formality level.
Introducing yourself
Use ser to state your name, origin, and basic identity.
The verb SER
Ser means to be for permanent qualities: identity, origin, and characteristic traits.
Formal vs informal address
Choosing tú or usted sets the social tone of the whole conversation.
The alphabet & pronunciation
Spanish spelling is phonetic — once you know the letters, you can say any word.
Key fact
Tú = informal, usted = formal — the verb form changes with the choice
Key fact
Ser is for permanent identity: origin, profession, personality, time
Key fact
H is silent; ñ is a separate letter (año ≠ ano)

Unit 2: Numbers, Dates & Time

Numbers 0–100
Spanish numbers follow patterns: memorize 0–15, then tens build predictably.
Number agreement
Uno and numbers ending in one shorten before masculine nouns.
Dates
Dates run day-month-year and use ser for the day and month names.
Telling time
Time uses ser + definite article: es la una / son las dos.
Classroom schedule talk
Combining days, times, and tener describes your weekly routine.
Key fact
Es la una (1:00) is singular; son las dos+ (plural) — time agrees like articles
Key fact
y media = :30, y cuarto = :15, menos cuarto = :45
Key fact
Only primero marks the 1st of a month; other dates use cardinal numbers

Unit 3: Family & Descriptions

Family vocabulary
Family terms in Spanish distinguish gender and include in-laws and step-relatives.
Possessive adjectives
Possessives agree with the THING owned, not the owner.
Descriptive adjectives & agreement
Adjectives copy the gender and number of the person they describe.
Physical & personality traits
Two adjective families describe people: looks and character.
Asking about family
Question words let you interview someone about their family.
Key fact
Possessives agree with the NOWNED thing: nuestra abuela, nuestros abuelos
Key fact
Su/sus covers his+her+their+your(formal) — context or de + name disambiguates
Key fact
Adjectives mirror gender AND number: primas simpáticas

Unit 4: School & -AR Verbs

School vocabulary
Classes, supplies, and places around campus.
Present tense -AR verbs
The largest verb family follows one clean pattern: drop -ar, add endings.
Using verbs in sentences
One conjugated verb carries a sentence; second actions stay infinitive.
-AR conjugation drills
Speed comes from pattern recognition, not memorizing each verb separately.
Key fact
Drop -AR, add: o/as/a/amos/áis/an — one template rules hundreds of verbs
Key fact
Él and usted share endings; ellos and ustedes share endings
Key fact
After a conjugated verb, the next verb stays infinitive: Necesito hablar con ella

Unit 5: Food & -ER/-IR Verbs

Food vocabulary
Meals and staples anchor daily conversation.
-ER verbs
Same idea as -AR, new endings: drop -er, add -o/-es/-e/-emos/-éis/-en.
-IR verbs
Identical to -ER except nosotros/vosotros: -imos / -ís.
Restaurant & café phrases
Ordering food politely uses querer + noun and account phrases.
Key fact
-ER endings: o/es/e/emos/éis/en · -IR differs only at -imos/-ís
Key fact
Compartir el almuerzo = to share lunch; almorzar stem-changes o→ue
Key fact
Con = with, sin = without — instant order customization

Unit 6: Gustar & Likes

How gustar actually works
Gustar means TO BE PLEASING — the thing liked is the subject, not you.
Gustar-family verbs
Other verbs work the same backward way.
Expressing degree
Dial likes up and down with adverbs and set phrases.
Activities & hobbies vocab
Pair gustar with infinitives and nouns for weekend talk.
Key fact
Gustar conjugates to match the THING: gusta (sing/infinitive), gustan (plural)
Key fact
A mí / A Juan clarifies WHO likes when le/les is ambiguous
Key fact
No me gusta nada = strongest negative; me gusta mucho = strongest positive

Unit 7: Stem-Changers & Clothing

E→IE boot verbs
The stressed e inside the stem swaps to ie in all forms except nosotros/vosotros.
O→UE and E→I boots
Two more swap patterns cover most remaining irregulars.
Clothing vocabulary
Wardrobe words pair with llevar for what someone wears.
Shopping & prices
Buying talk uses costar (o:ue), demonstratives, and numbers from Unit 2.
Key fact
Stem-changers skip nosotros/vosotros — that's why they're called BOOT verbs
Key fact
Three patterns: e→ie (querer), o→ue (poder), e→i (pedir); jugar alone goes u→ue
Key fact
Quedar = fit: Me quedan bien estos zapatos

Unit 8: House & Ser vs Estar

House vocabulary
Rooms and furniture describe where life happens.
ESTAR basics
Estar handles location and condition — the other to be.
SER vs ESTAR decision rule
One question settles it: permanent identity (ser) vs state/location right now (estar).
Prepositions of place
Small words locate everything in the house.
Key fact
Estar conjugates irregularly in YO only: estoy (the rest look normal)
Key fact
DOCTOR uses ser (Description, Occupation, Characteristic, Time, Origin, Relationship); PLACE uses estar (Position, Location, Action-in-progress, Condition, Emotion)
Key fact
de + el = del, a + el = al — the only mandatory contractions
Common mistakes for each unit — read the mistake, then make sure you know why it's wrong.

Unit 1: Greetings & Introductions

Watch out
¿Cómo estás? takes estás (estar), NOT eres — feelings use estar even though they seem temporary-permanent confusing
Watch out
Age uses TENER not ser: Tengo quince años, never Soy quince años
Watch out
Buenas noches covers BOTH evening and night — it's not only 'good night' at bedtime

Unit 2: Numbers, Dates & Time

Watch out
Es la UNA, not son las una — one o'clock breaks the plural rule
Watch out
Don't capitalize months/days: lunes, enero — unlike English
Watch out
31+ uses y (treinta y uno); 21-29 fuses (veintiuno) — mixing these up is the classic error

Unit 3: Family & Descriptions

Watch out
Mi hermano's possessive is MI not mi'o — short forms never take accents before the noun
Watch out
Tener hambre = to BE hungry, literally to HAVE hunger — no ser/estar involved
Watch out
Los padres means parents; saying los papás de mi papá needs abuelos instead

Unit 4: School & -AR Verbs

Watch out
Don't double-conjugate: Necesito estudio is wrong — Necesito ESTUDIAR
Watch out
The accent in habláis/estudiáis (vosotros) matters — hablais is misspelled
Watch out
Nunca can start the sentence (Nunca trabajo) but then NO doesn't repeat — Nunca trabajo, never No nunca trabajo

Unit 5: Food & -ER/-IR Verbs

Watch out
El agua is FEMININE despite el — it takes el for stress but adjectives stay feminine: el agua fría
Watch out
Fish swimming is el pez; fish on your plate is el pescado
Watch out
Don't use ser for hungry-thirsty: Tengo sed/hambre (tener), never Estoy sed

Unit 6: Gustar & Likes

Watch out
Me gusto means I please MYSELF — the pronoun must be indirect: ME GUSTA
Watch out
Two things liked still take plural: Me gustan el pan Y el queso
Watch out
Encantar ≠ weak: it's stronger than gustar (love ≫ like), not a synonym

Unit 7: Stem-Changers & Clothing

Watch out
Yo puedo but NOSOTROS podemos — never podimos in present tense
Watch out
Costar agrees with the ITEM not the buyer: Cuestan $20 (they cost), even if you say I want them
Watch out
Demonstrative este = near ME, ese = nearer YOU — mixing them confuses which item you're buying

Unit 8: House & Ser vs Estar

Watch out
LOCATIONS take estar EXCEPT event venues: la reunión ES en la biblioteca
Watch out
Estás aburrido (you're bored) vs eres aburrido (you're boring) — one adjective, two lives
Watch out
La nevera está en la cocina — never use ser for where furniture sits
