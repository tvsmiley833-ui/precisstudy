Unit 1: Mental & Emotional Health

Mental Health Continuum
A range from thriving to struggling that a person moves along over time, rather than a fixed 'healthy' or 'ill' label.
Eustress
Positive stress that can motivate performance and feels manageable, such as excitement before a big event.
Distress
Negative stress that overwhelms a person's ability to cope and can harm physical and mental health.
Fight-or-Flight Response
The body's automatic physical reaction to a perceived threat, releasing adrenaline and cortisol to prepare for quick action.
Emotional Intelligence (EQ)
The ability to recognize, understand, and manage your own emotions while recognizing and responding to others' emotions.
Resilience
The ability to adapt to and recover from adversity, setbacks, or stress; a skill that can be strengthened over time.
Depression
A mental health disorder involving persistent sadness or loss of interest lasting two weeks or more, affecting daily functioning.
Generalized Anxiety Disorder
A disorder involving excessive, hard-to-control worry most days for six months or more that interferes with daily life.
Stigma
Negative stereotypes or judgment about a condition (such as mental illness) that discourages people from seeking help.
988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline
A free, confidential U.S. hotline available 24/7 by call, text, or chat for anyone in emotional distress or suicidal crisis.

Unit 2: Nutrition & Physical Fitness

Macronutrients
Nutrients needed in large amounts for energy and growth: carbohydrates, proteins, and fats.
Micronutrients
Vitamins and minerals needed in small amounts that support functions like immunity and bone health.
MyPlate
The USDA's visual guide for balanced eating, dividing a plate into fruits, vegetables, grains, and protein, plus dairy.
Cardiorespiratory Endurance
The heart and lungs' ability to supply oxygen to the body during sustained physical activity.
FITT Principle
A framework for designing exercise plans based on Frequency, Intensity, Time, and Type.
Muscular Endurance
A muscle's ability to perform repeated contractions over time without becoming fatigued.
Body Composition
The ratio of fat mass to lean mass (muscle, bone, organs) that make up total body weight.
Anorexia Nervosa
An eating disorder involving severe food restriction driven by an intense fear of gaining weight and distorted body image.
Bulimia Nervosa
An eating disorder involving cycles of binge eating followed by purging behaviors like vomiting or excessive exercise.
Binge Eating Disorder
An eating disorder involving recurrent episodes of eating large amounts of food with a felt loss of control, without purging.

Unit 3: Substance Abuse Prevention

Depressant
A drug category that slows central nervous system activity, causing relaxation, slowed breathing, and impaired coordination (e.g., alcohol, opioids).
Stimulant
A drug category that speeds up central nervous system activity, increasing heart rate and alertness (e.g., nicotine, cocaine).
Tolerance
The need for increasing amounts of a substance over time to achieve the same effect.
Physical Dependence
A state in which the body has adapted to a substance and experiences withdrawal symptoms without it.
Blood Alcohol Concentration (BAC)
The percentage of alcohol present in a person's bloodstream, which rises with each drink consumed.
Binge Drinking
Consuming 4+ drinks (females) or 5+ drinks (males) within about a two-hour period, sharply raising alcohol poisoning risk.
Fentanyl
A powerful synthetic opioid increasingly mixed into other drugs, dramatically increasing overdose risk even in tiny amounts.
Naloxone (Narcan)
A medication that can rapidly reverse the effects of an opioid overdose when administered in time.
Refusal Skills
Communication strategies — like clearly saying no and suggesting an alternative — used to resist peer pressure to use substances.
Addiction
A chronic brain disease involving compulsive substance use despite harmful consequences, driven by changes to the brain's dopamine reward system.

Unit 4: Human Development

Puberty
The biological process of sexual and physical maturation triggered by hormones, typically occurring between ages 8-14.
Menstrual Cycle
A roughly 28-day hormonal cycle involving ovulation and, without fertilization, the shedding of the uterine lining.
Ovulation
The release of a mature egg from the ovary, usually near the midpoint of the menstrual cycle.
Fertilization
The joining of a sperm cell and an egg cell, typically occurring in the fallopian tube.
Trimester
One of three roughly 13-week periods used to divide the approximately 40 weeks of pregnancy.
Prenatal Care
Regular medical checkups during pregnancy that monitor the health of both mother and developing baby.
Fetal Alcohol Spectrum Disorder (FASD)
A group of lifelong physical and cognitive conditions caused by alcohol exposure during pregnancy.
Sexually Transmitted Infection (STI)
An infection passed primarily through sexual contact; many, like chlamydia and HPV, often show no symptoms.
Abstinence
Choosing not to have sex; the only method that is 100% effective at preventing both pregnancy and STI transmission.
Contraception
Methods used to prevent pregnancy, ranging from barrier methods to hormonal methods, none 100% effective except abstinence.

Unit 5: Injury Prevention & Safety

RICE method
Rest, Ice, Compression, Elevation — the standard first 24-48 hour treatment for sprains and strains.
Sprain
A stretched or torn ligament, the tissue connecting bone to bone.
Strain
A stretched or torn muscle or tendon, the tissue connecting muscle to bone.
Hands-only CPR
Chest compressions at 100-120 per minute for an unresponsive adult not breathing normally, without rescue breaths.
AED (automated external defibrillator)
A device that analyzes heart rhythm and delivers a shock only if a shockable rhythm is detected; it guides the user through each step.
Heimlich maneuver
Abdominal thrusts used to dislodge an object from the airway of a person who is choking and cannot cough, speak, or breathe.
Heat stroke
A life-threatening emergency involving high body temperature, confusion, and hot, dry skin; requires immediate cooling and 911.
Distracted driving
Any activity (texting, eating, adjusting music) that diverts attention from driving; hands-free use is not fully safe either.
Good Samaritan laws
Laws that generally protect bystanders who give reasonable emergency help in good faith from being sued.
Chain of Survival
The sequence of early recognition/911, early CPR, early defibrillation, and early advanced care that improves cardiac arrest survival.

Unit 6: Disease Prevention

Communicable disease
A disease caused by a pathogen that can spread from person to person, like the flu.
Noncommunicable disease
A disease not spread person-to-person, usually caused by genetics, lifestyle, or environment, like heart disease.
Pathogen
A microorganism (bacteria, virus, fungus, parasite) capable of causing disease.
Antibody
A protein made by white blood cells that recognizes and binds to a specific pathogen, marking it for destruction.
Herd immunity
Protection that occurs when enough of a population is immune, slowing disease spread and protecting those who can't be vaccinated.
Vaccine
A weakened, inactivated, or partial form of a pathogen given to build immune memory without causing the disease.
Atherosclerosis
A buildup of plaque that narrows arteries, a major cause of cardiovascular disease.
Modifiable risk factor
A disease risk factor a person can change, such as diet, physical activity, or tobacco use.
Epidemic vs. pandemic
An epidemic is a disease spreading rapidly in a specific area; a pandemic is a global outbreak.
Universal precautions
Practices like wearing gloves and avoiding contact with blood or bodily fluids to prevent infection when giving first aid.

Unit 7: Consumer & Community Health

Health fraud (quackery)
Selling products or services using false or exaggerated health claims, often targeting people seeking a quick fix.
Premium
The regular amount paid for health insurance, regardless of whether care is used that period.
Deductible
The amount a person must pay out-of-pocket for covered services before insurance starts paying its share.
Copay vs. coinsurance
A copay is a fixed fee for a service; coinsurance is a percentage of the cost shared between patient and insurer.
Primary care provider
A healthcare provider who manages overall health, routine checkups, and referrals to specialists.
CDC
The Centers for Disease Control and Prevention — tracks disease outbreaks and issues public health guidance in the U.S.
FDA
The Food and Drug Administration — regulates the safety of food, drugs, vaccines, and medical devices.
Food desert
An area with limited access to affordable, healthy food, linked to poorer diet quality and higher chronic disease rates.
Environmental justice
The recognition that pollution and health hazards are often unevenly distributed, disproportionately affecting lower-income communities.
Health advocacy
Taking action to support health at a personal, community, or policy level, such as supporting a public health law.

Unit 8: Healthy Relationships

Healthy relationship
A relationship built on mutual respect, trust, honesty, equality, and support for each person's individuality.
'I' statement
A way of expressing a concern (e.g., 'I feel frustrated when...') that avoids blame and reduces defensiveness.
Consent (F.R.I.E.S.)
Agreement that must be Freely given, Reversible, Informed, Enthusiastic, and Specific.
Boundary
A physical, emotional, or digital limit a person sets for what they are and are not comfortable with.
Cycle of abuse
A pattern of tension-building, an abusive incident, and a 'honeymoon' reconciliation phase that repeats.
Digital abuse
Controlling behavior through technology, such as demanding passwords or constantly monitoring someone's messages.
Assertive communication
Expressing your own needs and opinions clearly and respectfully, without being passive or aggressive.
Refusal skills
Techniques like saying no clearly, offering an alternative, and being willing to leave a situation to resist pressure.
Peer pressure
Influence from peers to think or act a certain way; can be direct or indirect, positive or negative.
National Domestic Violence Hotline
A confidential resource available to anyone in, or worried about, an abusive relationship.

Unit 1: Mental & Emotional Health

The Mental Health Continuum
Mental health is not a fixed label of 'healthy' or 'ill' — it exists on a continuum from thriving to struggling, and everyone moves along it over time depending on life circumstances, coping skills, and support.
Stress and the Body's Stress Response
Stress is the body's physical and emotional reaction to a demand or challenge. The 'fight-or-flight' response releases adrenaline and cortisol to prepare the body to react quickly, which is helpful short-term but harmful if triggered constantly.
Emotions and Emotional Intelligence
Emotional intelligence (EQ) is the ability to recognize, understand, and manage your own emotions while also recognizing and responding well to the emotions of others.
Healthy Coping Strategies and Resilience
Coping strategies are the specific actions people use to manage stress and difficult emotions. Resilience is the ability to adapt and bounce back after setbacks, and it can be built like a skill.
Anxiety and Depressive Disorders
Anxiety disorders and depression are the most common mental health conditions among teens. They are medical conditions involving changes in brain chemistry and function, not a choice or character flaw.
Warning Signs and Seeking Help
Knowing the warning signs of a mental health crisis and how to respond can save a life. Suicide is preventable, and most people who consider it give some warning signs beforehand.
Key fact
About 1 in 5 teens experiences a diagnosable mental health disorder in a given year, most commonly anxiety or depression
Key fact
Chronic (long-term) stress raises cortisol levels and is linked to high blood pressure, weakened immunity, and disrupted sleep
Key fact
Depression is diagnosed when symptoms last two weeks or more and interfere with daily functioning, not just a bad day
Key fact
988 (call, text, or chat) is the free, confidential U.S. Suicide & Crisis Lifeline available 24 hours a day

Unit 2: Nutrition & Physical Fitness

Macronutrients and Their Roles
Macronutrients are the nutrients the body needs in large amounts for energy and growth: carbohydrates, proteins, and fats. Each has a distinct job in the body.
Micronutrients: Vitamins and Minerals
Micronutrients are vitamins and minerals needed in small amounts that support essential body functions like immunity, bone health, and energy metabolism.
MyPlate and Balanced Eating
MyPlate is the USDA's visual guide for building balanced meals, replacing the older food pyramid, and emphasizing portion balance across food groups.
Components of Physical Fitness
Physical fitness has five key components, and a well-rounded fitness plan addresses all of them rather than just one.
Benefits of Regular Physical Activity
Regular physical activity benefits nearly every body system and is one of the most powerful tools for long-term health, both physical and mental.
Body Image, Eating Disorders, and Healthy Weight Management
A healthy relationship with food and body image is essential; when it breaks down, it can lead to disordered eating patterns that require professional treatment.
Key fact
MyPlate recommends filling half the plate with fruits and vegetables at most meals
Key fact
Carbohydrates and protein each provide about 4 calories per gram; fat provides about 9 calories per gram
Key fact
The CDC recommends at least 60 minutes of moderate-to-vigorous physical activity daily for teens
Key fact
The five components of fitness are cardiorespiratory endurance, muscular strength, muscular endurance, flexibility, and body composition

Unit 3: Substance Abuse Prevention

Categories of Drugs and How They Affect the Body
Drugs are classified by how they affect the central nervous system: depressants, stimulants, and hallucinogens each produce different effects on mood, perception, and body function.
Alcohol: Effects and Risks
Alcohol is a depressant that impairs judgment, coordination, and reaction time, and its effects are especially harmful to the developing adolescent brain.
Nicotine, Vaping, and Tobacco
Nicotine is a highly addictive stimulant found in cigarettes, e-cigarettes/vapes, and other tobacco products, and it is especially harmful to the developing adolescent brain.
Marijuana and Other Illicit Drugs
Marijuana (cannabis) and other illicit drugs carry health and legal risks, and their effects vary widely by substance, dose, and the user's age.
Why People Use Substances and Risk/Protective Factors
Understanding why people begin using substances helps identify effective prevention strategies, since risk rarely comes from a single cause.
Addiction as a Disease and Paths to Recovery
Addiction is recognized by medical science as a chronic disease of the brain's reward system, not a moral failing, and recovery is possible with proper treatment and support.
Key fact
Nicotine reaches the brain within seconds and is highly addictive, especially for the developing teen brain
Key fact
Binge drinking is defined as 4+ drinks for females or 5+ for males within about two hours
Key fact
Fentanyl is a powerful synthetic opioid increasingly mixed into other drugs, greatly increasing overdose risk
Key fact
Addiction is classified as a chronic brain disease affecting the dopamine reward system, not a moral failing

Unit 4: Human Development

Puberty and Adolescent Development
Puberty is the biological process during which the body matures sexually and physically, triggered by hormones, and it brings physical, emotional, and social changes.
The Reproductive Systems
The male and female reproductive systems each have specific organs and functions related to producing offspring, and understanding them is essential for making informed health decisions.
Pregnancy and Prenatal Development
Pregnancy involves the development of a fertilized egg into a fetus over approximately 40 weeks, divided into three trimesters, each with distinct developmental milestones.
Sexually Transmitted Infections and Prevention
Sexually transmitted infections (STIs) are infections passed primarily through sexual contact, and understanding transmission and prevention is a core part of reproductive health education.
Contraception and Responsible Decision-Making
Contraception refers to methods used to prevent pregnancy, and understanding the range of options — along with their effectiveness — supports informed, responsible decision-making.
Family Life, Parenting Readiness, and Life Stages
Human development continues across the lifespan, and understanding the stages of life — along with the responsibilities of parenting — helps build realistic expectations for the future.
Key fact
Puberty is triggered by hormones — estrogen/progesterone in females and testosterone in males — and timing varies normally between individuals
Key fact
Pregnancy lasts about 40 weeks, divided into three trimesters, each with distinct developmental milestones
Key fact
Abstinence is the only method that is 100% effective at preventing both pregnancy and STI transmission
Key fact
Many STIs, including chlamydia and HPV, often show no symptoms, making regular testing essential for sexually active people

Unit 5: Injury Prevention & Safety

Treating Common Injuries: RICE and Beyond
Most sports and activity injuries — sprains, strains, and minor fractures — respond well to prompt, correct first aid, and knowing the difference between injury types determines the right response.
CPR, AEDs, and Responding to Choking
Cardiac arrest and choking are life-threatening emergencies where bystander action in the first few minutes dramatically affects survival, and basic steps can be learned by anyone.
Motor Vehicle and Pedestrian Safety
Motor vehicle crashes are the leading cause of death for U.S. teenagers, and most of the risk factors involved are preventable through specific behavior changes.
Water, Weather, and Recreational Safety
Environmental hazards like water, extreme heat, extreme cold, and severe weather cause preventable injuries and deaths every year, especially during outdoor recreation.
Violence Prevention and Personal Safety
Preventing violence — from bullying to weapons-related incidents — relies on recognizing warning signs early, defusing conflict, and knowing when and how to get help.
Emergency Preparedness and the EMS System
Knowing how to activate the emergency medical system (EMS) and prepare for likely local disasters turns a bystander into an effective first responder.
Key fact
Motor vehicle crashes are a leading cause of death among U.S. teenagers, and seatbelt use cuts fatal injury risk roughly in half
Key fact
Hands-only CPR uses chest compressions at a rate of 100-120 per minute for an unresponsive adult who isn't breathing normally
Key fact
RICE (Rest, Ice, Compression, Elevation) is the standard first 24-48 hour treatment for sprains and strains
Key fact
Heat stroke — confusion with hot, dry skin and no sweating — is a life-threatening emergency requiring immediate cooling and 911

Unit 6: Disease Prevention

Communicable vs. Noncommunicable Disease
Diseases fall into two broad categories based on whether they can spread from person to person, and the prevention strategy for each is very different.
How Pathogens Spread and the Immune System's Defense
Understanding how pathogens travel from person to person, and how the immune system fights them off, explains why hygiene practices and vaccines work.
Vaccines and Herd Immunity
Vaccines train the immune system to recognize a pathogen without causing the disease itself, and when enough of a population is vaccinated, it protects even those who cannot be.
Cardiovascular Disease and Cancer Risk Factors
Cardiovascular disease and cancer are leading noncommunicable causes of death, and many of their risk factors are modifiable through lifestyle choices.
Chronic Disease Prevention Through Lifestyle
Many of the most common chronic diseases share the same small set of preventable lifestyle risk factors, which means the same healthy habits protect against multiple diseases at once.
Hygiene Practices That Prevent Illness
Simple, consistent hygiene habits break the chain of infection at the point of transmission and are among the most effective, low-cost tools for preventing communicable disease.
Key fact
Communicable diseases are caused by pathogens and spread between people; noncommunicable diseases are not spread person-to-person
Key fact
Vaccines build immune memory using a weakened, inactivated, or partial pathogen so the body can respond quickly to a real future infection
Key fact
Herd immunity protects people who cannot be vaccinated when enough of the surrounding population is immune
Key fact
Handwashing with soap for at least 20 seconds is one of the single most effective ways to prevent the spread of illness

Unit 7: Consumer & Community Health

Being a Critical Health Consumer
Health consumers are constantly targeted by advertising and health claims, and evaluating those claims critically protects both wallets and well-being.
Understanding Health Insurance and Healthcare Costs
Health insurance is a system for sharing the financial risk of medical costs, and understanding its basic vocabulary helps people make informed healthcare decisions.
Choosing Healthcare Providers and Services
Different healthcare needs call for different types of providers and settings, and knowing which to use saves time, money, and gets more appropriate care.
Public Health Agencies and Their Roles
A network of government agencies at the local, national, and global level works to protect community health through research, regulation, and response.
Environmental Health and Community Resources
The environment where people live, work, and go to school directly shapes health outcomes, and communities can act collectively to reduce environmental health risks.
Health Advocacy and Community Health Promotion
Health advocacy means taking action to support health at a personal, community, or policy level, and it's a skill anyone can build regardless of age.
Key fact
A premium is what you pay for insurance regardless of use; a deductible is what you pay out-of-pocket before insurance coverage kicks in
Key fact
The CDC tracks disease and issues guidance nationally, the FDA regulates food/drug/device safety, and the WHO coordinates global public health
Key fact
Dietary supplements are regulated far less strictly by the FDA than prescription or over-the-counter medications
Key fact
Urgent care is for prompt, non-life-threatening issues; the emergency room is reserved for life-threatening conditions

Unit 8: Healthy Relationships

Characteristics of Healthy vs. Unhealthy Relationships
All relationships — friendships, family, and romantic — fall somewhere on a spectrum from healthy to unhealthy to abusive, and recognizing the pattern matters more than any single incident.
Communication Skills and Conflict Resolution
Conflict is a normal part of any relationship; what determines whether a relationship stays healthy is how the people involved communicate through it.
Setting Boundaries and Understanding Consent
Boundaries are the limits a person sets for what they are and are not comfortable with, and consent is the clear, ongoing agreement required before any shared activity, especially physical intimacy.
Recognizing and Responding to Abuse
Abuse in a relationship can be physical, emotional, sexual, or digital, and recognizing early warning signs — in your own relationship or a friend's — is critical to getting help before harm escalates.
Peer Pressure and Assertiveness
Peers strongly influence adolescent decision-making, and assertive communication is the key skill that allows someone to stay true to their values under social pressure.
Family Relationships and Support Systems
Family relationships, whatever form they take, are often a person's first and most enduring support system, and the same principles of healthy communication apply within them.
Key fact
Consent must be freely given, reversible, informed, enthusiastic, and specific — often remembered with the acronym F.R.I.E.S.
Key fact
The cycle of abuse typically includes tension-building, an incident, and a 'honeymoon' reconciliation phase, which can make leaving feel confusing
Key fact
Assertive communication expresses your needs clearly and respectfully, distinct from passive (avoiding conflict) or aggressive (disrespecting others) styles
Key fact
'I' statements reduce defensiveness in conflict by expressing a feeling without directly blaming the other person

Unit 1: Mental & Emotional Health

Watch out
All stress is harmful, not true — eustress (like excitement before a big game) is short-term stress that can improve focus and performance
Watch out
Mental illness is a sign of personal weakness, not true — it is a medical condition involving brain chemistry, genetics, and environment, just like a physical illness
Watch out
Asking someone directly if they're suicidal will give them the idea, not true — direct, caring questions open the door to help and do not increase risk
Watch out
Keeping a friend's suicidal thoughts secret is loyalty, not true — telling a trusted adult immediately is the responsible, life-saving action

Unit 2: Nutrition & Physical Fitness

Watch out
'Fat-free' means healthy, not true — fat-free foods often add sugar to make up for lost flavor, so the label alone doesn't guarantee it's healthier
Watch out
Skipping meals is an effective way to lose weight, not true — it often leads to overeating later and can slow metabolism
Watch out
Eating disorders are a lifestyle choice about vanity, not true — they are serious mental illnesses with biological, psychological, and social roots
Watch out
All fats are bad for you, not true — unsaturated fats (nuts, olive oil, fish) support heart health, while saturated/trans fats in excess raise risk

Unit 3: Substance Abuse Prevention

Watch out
Vaping is a harmless alternative to smoking, not true — vapes deliver addictive nicotine plus heated chemicals and are not proven safe
Watch out
Marijuana is completely harmless because it's 'natural,' not true — THC impairs memory, coordination, and judgment, especially in the developing teen brain
Watch out
Addiction is just a lack of willpower, not true — it is a chronic brain disease that changes the dopamine reward pathway and requires treatment
Watch out
Prescription drugs are always safe because a doctor prescribes them, not true — taking them differently than prescribed or using someone else's prescription carries real risks

Unit 4: Human Development

Watch out
You can tell if someone has an STI just by looking at them, not true — many STIs show no visible symptoms at all
Watch out
Birth control pills protect against STIs, not true — hormonal methods prevent pregnancy but provide no protection against sexually transmitted infections
Watch out
Puberty happens at the exact same age and pace for everyone, not true — timing and pace vary widely and normally between individuals
Watch out
A condom is 100% effective at preventing pregnancy and STIs, not true — used correctly it greatly reduces risk, but only abstinence is 100% effective

Unit 5: Injury Prevention & Safety

Watch out
Hands-free texting or calls while driving are just as safe as not using a phone, not true — both divert attention and slow reaction time similarly to handheld use
Watch out
You should always try to straighten or realign a suspected broken bone, not true — the area should be immobilized as found, not straightened
Watch out
Applying ice directly to bare skin speeds healing, not true — direct ice contact for extended periods can cause frostbite; always use a cloth barrier
Watch out
Drowning always looks dramatic, with lots of splashing and yelling, not true — it is often silent and quick, which is why constant supervision near water is essential

Unit 6: Disease Prevention

Watch out
Vaccines can give you the disease they protect against, not true — mild side effects like soreness or low fever are signs of an immune response, not illness
Watch out
If you have no family history of a disease, lifestyle habits don't matter, not true — modifiable risk factors like diet, activity, and smoking affect risk regardless of genetics
Watch out
Antibiotics work against viruses like the cold or flu, not true — antibiotics only treat bacterial infections, not viral ones
Watch out
You can't spread an illness if you feel fine, not true — many pathogens are contagious during an incubation period before symptoms appear

Unit 7: Consumer & Community Health

Watch out
'Natural' or 'FDA-approved' on a supplement label guarantees it's safe and effective, not true — supplements face much lighter regulation than medications
Watch out
Going to the ER for any medical concern is always the right call, not true — non-emergencies are better and more affordably handled at urgent care or a primary provider
Watch out
A higher premium always means better coverage, not true — plans vary widely in deductibles, copays, and networks, so total cost depends on the whole plan
Watch out
Public health agencies only matter during a crisis, not true — they do ongoing work like restaurant inspections, immunization programs, and water quality monitoring year-round

Unit 8: Healthy Relationships

Watch out
If someone doesn't say 'no,' that means they've consented, not true — consent requires clear, active, enthusiastic agreement, not just the absence of refusal
Watch out
Jealousy is proof that a partner really cares, not true — extreme jealousy and controlling behavior are warning signs of an unhealthy or abusive dynamic, not love
Watch out
Leaving an abusive relationship is simple once you decide to do it, not true — leaving is often the most dangerous period, which is why safety planning with help matters
Watch out
Arguing means a relationship is failing, not true — conflict is normal in any relationship; what matters is whether it's handled with respect and resolution
