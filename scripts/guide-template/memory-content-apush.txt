
  <div class="qr-section"><h2 class="qr-h">Unit 1: Period 1: 1491-1607</h2><div class="mem-grid">
    <div class="mem-card"><div class="mem-t">Watch out</div><div class="mem-d">Native American societies were NOT uniform — economy and social structure varied hugely by region/environment.</div></div>
    <div class="mem-card"><div class="mem-t">Watch out</div><div class="mem-d">Disease, not superior weaponry alone, caused most Native depopulation after contact.</div></div>
    <div class="mem-card"><div class="mem-t">Watch out</div><div class="mem-d">The encomienda system was a form of forced labor, not free wage labor, despite the 'protection' rhetoric.</div></div>
    <div class="mem-card"><div class="mem-t">Watch out</div><div class="mem-d">Columbus reached the Caribbean in 1492, not the mainland North American colonies that came later.</div></div>
  </div></div>
  <div class="qr-section"><h2 class="qr-h">Unit 2: Period 2: 1607-1754</h2><div class="mem-grid">
    <div class="mem-card"><div class="mem-t">Watch out</div><div class="mem-d">New England was founded for religious purposes; the Chesapeake was founded primarily for profit (tobacco).</div></div>
    <div class="mem-card"><div class="mem-t">Watch out</div><div class="mem-d">Bacon's Rebellion (1676) accelerated the shift from indentured servitude to racial slavery — it did not cause slavery to begin.</div></div>
    <div class="mem-card"><div class="mem-t">Watch out</div><div class="mem-d">Pennsylvania's religious tolerance under Penn was unusually broad for its time, not the colonial norm.</div></div>
    <div class="mem-card"><div class="mem-t">Watch out</div><div class="mem-d">Salutary neglect describes lax enforcement BEFORE 1754 — not a permanent British policy.</div></div>
  </div></div>
  <div class="qr-section"><h2 class="qr-h">Unit 3: Period 3: 1754-1800</h2><div class="mem-grid">
    <div class="mem-card"><div class="mem-t">Watch out</div><div class="mem-d">The Three-Fifths Compromise was about representation/taxation, not about the moral status of slavery itself.</div></div>
    <div class="mem-card"><div class="mem-t">Watch out</div><div class="mem-d">The Articles of Confederation failed due to WEAKNESS (no taxing/military power), not because it was too strong.</div></div>
    <div class="mem-card"><div class="mem-t">Watch out</div><div class="mem-d">Anti-Federalists opposed ratification of the Constitution — they did not oppose independence from Britain.</div></div>
    <div class="mem-card"><div class="mem-t">Watch out</div><div class="mem-d">Hamilton favored loose construction and a strong federal government; Jefferson favored strict construction and states' rights — it's easy to mix these up.</div></div>
  </div></div>
  <div class="qr-section"><h2 class="qr-h">Unit 4: Period 4: 1800-1848</h2><div class="mem-grid">
    <div class="mem-card"><div class="mem-t">Watch out</div><div class="mem-d">Jacksonian 'democracy' expanded suffrage for white men while intensifying the removal and disenfranchisement of Native Americans.</div></div>
    <div class="mem-card"><div class="mem-t">Watch out</div><div class="mem-d">The Second Great Awakening's religious revivalism directly fueled reform movements like abolition and temperance — the two are linked, not separate.</div></div>
    <div class="mem-card"><div class="mem-t">Watch out</div><div class="mem-d">The Wilmot Proviso never passed Congress, but it foreshadowed the sectional slavery debate of the 1850s.</div></div>
    <div class="mem-card"><div class="mem-t">Watch out</div><div class="mem-d">Manifest Destiny is a mid-1840s term/ideology, not a formal government policy from the Founding era.</div></div>
  </div></div>
  <div class="qr-section"><h2 class="qr-h">Unit 5: Period 5: 1844-1877</h2><div class="mem-grid">
    <div class="mem-card"><div class="mem-t">Watch out</div><div class="mem-d">The Emancipation Proclamation freed enslaved people only in Confederate-held territory — not in the loyal border states.</div></div>
    <div class="mem-card"><div class="mem-t">Watch out</div><div class="mem-d">Popular sovereignty (Kansas-Nebraska Act) let TERRITORIES vote on slavery — it did not resolve the debate, it caused violence.</div></div>
    <div class="mem-card"><div class="mem-t">Watch out</div><div class="mem-d">Reconstruction ended in 1877 due to a political compromise, not because racial equality had been achieved.</div></div>
    <div class="mem-card"><div class="mem-t">Watch out</div><div class="mem-d">The 13th, 14th, and 15th Amendments were passed DURING Reconstruction, not immediately in 1865 all at once.</div></div>
  </div></div>
  <div class="qr-section"><h2 class="qr-h">Unit 6: Period 6: 1865-1898</h2><div class="mem-grid">
    <div class="mem-card"><div class="mem-t">Watch out</div><div class="mem-d">Vertical integration (Carnegie, controlling supply chain) differs from horizontal integration (Rockefeller, buying out competitors) — don't confuse the two.</div></div>
    <div class="mem-card"><div class="mem-t">Watch out</div><div class="mem-d">The Knights of Labor were broadly inclusive; the AFL organized only skilled workers — a key contrast.</div></div>
    <div class="mem-card"><div class="mem-t">Watch out</div><div class="mem-d">'New immigrants' (Southern/Eastern Europe, after ~1880s) differ from earlier 'old immigrants' (Northern/Western Europe) — nativist reaction targeted the newer group.</div></div>
    <div class="mem-card"><div class="mem-t">Watch out</div><div class="mem-d">Populism was primarily an agrarian (farmer) movement, distinct from the urban, worker-focused labor movement, though both arose from industrial-era grievances.</div></div>
  </div></div>
  <div class="qr-section"><h2 class="qr-h">Unit 7: Period 7: 1890-1945</h2><div class="mem-grid">
    <div class="mem-card"><div class="mem-t">Watch out</div><div class="mem-d">Progressives sought to REGULATE big business (trust-busting, oversight), not necessarily abolish capitalism.</div></div>
    <div class="mem-card"><div class="mem-t">Watch out</div><div class="mem-d">The U.S. rejected the Treaty of Versailles/League of Nations due to Senate opposition, even though Wilson championed it.</div></div>
    <div class="mem-card"><div class="mem-t">Watch out</div><div class="mem-d">The New Deal did not end the Great Depression — WWII mobilization did, through massive government spending and full employment.</div></div>
    <div class="mem-card"><div class="mem-t">Watch out</div><div class="mem-d">Japanese American internment targeted citizens and immigrants alike based on ancestry, not on any evidence of individual disloyalty.</div></div>
  </div></div>
  <div class="qr-section"><h2 class="qr-h">Unit 8: Period 8: 1945-1980</h2><div class="mem-grid">
    <div class="mem-card"><div class="mem-t">Watch out</div><div class="mem-d">Containment aimed to STOP the spread of communism, not to roll back or defeat the USSR directly (that was 'rollback,' a more aggressive minority view).</div></div>
    <div class="mem-card"><div class="mem-t">Watch out</div><div class="mem-d">Brown v. Board (1954) addressed school segregation specifically; broader public accommodations discrimination was addressed later by the Civil Rights Act of 1964.</div></div>
    <div class="mem-card"><div class="mem-t">Watch out</div><div class="mem-d">The Tet Offensive was a military defeat for the communist forces but a psychological/political turning point against U.S. public support for the war.</div></div>
    <div class="mem-card"><div class="mem-t">Watch out</div><div class="mem-d">Détente eased tensions with the USSR/China but did NOT end the Cold War — it continued into the 1980s.</div></div>
  </div></div>
  <div class="qr-section"><h2 class="qr-h">Unit 9: Period 9: 1980-present</h2><div class="mem-grid">
    <div class="mem-card"><div class="mem-t">Watch out</div><div class="mem-d">Reaganomics called for smaller government, yet federal deficits and debt actually GREW during his presidency due to tax cuts plus increased defense spending.</div></div>
    <div class="mem-card"><div class="mem-t">Watch out</div><div class="mem-d">The Soviet Union's collapse (1991) resulted from internal economic weakness and Gorbachev's reforms, not from a single U.S. military victory.</div></div>
    <div class="mem-card"><div class="mem-t">Watch out</div><div class="mem-d">NAFTA (1994) is a free-trade agreement among the U.S., Canada, and Mexico — not a global or worldwide trade organization.</div></div>
    <div class="mem-card"><div class="mem-t">Watch out</div><div class="mem-d">The Great Recession began with the 2008 financial crisis, rooted in the housing/mortgage market and financial deregulation, not a stock market bubble alone like 1929.</div></div>
  </div></div>
